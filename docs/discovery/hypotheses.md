# Hypotheses to test

None of these has been tested. They are what this prototype assumes, written so an
interview can prove each one wrong. Update the status column as evidence comes in, and
link to the interview notes behind it.

| # | Hypothesis | What would disprove it | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| H1 | Small IT service firms (5 to 30 staff) make several quotes a week for small, repeatable jobs (device setup, network drops, onboarding). | Most quotes are large one-off projects, or there are only a few a month. | Untested |
| H2 | Turning a customer's email into a quote takes meaningful staff time, mostly in reading, asking follow-up questions and looking up prices. | Quotes take a few minutes, or the time goes into site surveys and design rather than paperwork. | Untested |
| H3 | Mistakes happen: wrong quantity, wrong site, forgotten trip fee, outdated price. | Firms can't recall quoting mistakes, or they don't matter because jobs are re-priced at invoicing. | Untested |
| H4 | A second person approves quotes above some amount, or would like to. | Owners quote and approve everything themselves and don't want a step added. | Untested |
| H5 | Firms keep a price list (spreadsheet or PSA tool) that could be imported. | Prices are mostly in someone's head, or every job is priced from scratch. | Untested |
| H6 | Firms would let software read customer requests, if it only proposes and a person decides. | Customer contracts or trust concerns rule this out. | Untested |
| H7 | Their PSA or ticketing tool doesn't already do this well enough. | Their current tool already turns requests into quotes and they're happy with it. | Untested |
| H8 | A firm would pay for this. | No firm will name a price or a budget line, even for a pilot. | Untested |

Rules for using this table:

- One interview is an anecdote. Note how many firms said what, and of what size.
- Record what people *did* last time (the last quote they made), not what they say they
  would do.
- "Sounds useful" is not evidence for H8. A committed pilot, a budget owner, or a price
  they would pay is.
