# Interview guide

For 30 to 45 minute conversations with owners, operations managers or service
coordinators at IT service firms with roughly 5 to 30 staff. The aim is to learn how they
quote today, not to sell the prototype. Don't show the app until the last ten minutes, if
at all.

## Before the call

- Note the firm's size, main services, and what tools they use, if public.
- Have [notes-template.md](notes-template.md) open.
- Agree up front how long the call takes.

## Consent (read out at the start)

> Thanks for making time. I'm researching how small IT service firms turn customer
> requests into quotes. I'm not selling anything today. I'd like to take notes; I won't
> record audio unless you agree. I'll keep your name and your firm's name out of anything
> I share, and I won't use what you tell me for anything else. You can skip any question or
> stop at any time. Is that OK?

Write down whether they agreed, and whether you may contact them again.

## Questions

Ask about the most recent real example wherever you can ("the last time that happened").

**Their firm**

1. How many people work there, and in what roles?
2. What kinds of work make up most of your jobs?
3. Roughly how many quotes or estimates did you send last month?

**The last quote**

4. Tell me about the last quote you sent. How did the request arrive?
5. What happened from the moment it arrived until the customer had a price? Who did what?
6. Did you need to go back to the customer for anything? What was missing?
7. How long did it take in total, and how much of that was waiting?
8. Where did the prices come from?

**Mistakes and approval**

9. Think of a time a quote went out wrong. What happened, and what did it cost you?
10. Does anyone check a quote before it goes out? When, and why (or why not)?

**Tools**

11. What do you use today for tickets, quotes and invoices?
12. What have you tried to make quoting faster? What happened?

**Software reading requests**

13. If software read the customer's email and suggested the quote, what would worry you?
14. Would any customer contracts stop you from doing that?

**Value**

15. If quoting took half the time, what would you do with the time?
16. Who would decide on buying a tool like this, and what do you pay for similar tools?

**Close**

17. Is there anything I should have asked?
18. Could you share a few real requests, with personal details removed, so I can test
    against real wording? (If yes, explain [the rules for real data](README.md#rules-for-real-data)
    and agree it in writing.)
19. Who else should I talk to?

## After the call

- Fill in the notes template the same day, with quotes in their words.
- Add a row to [interview-tracker.csv](interview-tracker.csv).
- Update [hypotheses.md](hypotheses.md) only with what they described doing, not opinions.
